AP Chemistry application units 4/5 to VLSI and Chip Design

A connection between unit 5 of AP Chemistry and VLSI would be in designing sub-threshold (consume minimal battery thereby exponentially reducing the need to replace batteries for heart implanted chips) AI chips.

Unit 5 of AP Chem, which focuses on kinetics, deals with reaction rates and collision theory, which are useful in determining the behavior of ISFET (a type of MOSFET sensor) with blood in the body.

**This predicted behavior is crucial and requires immense, near perfect accuracy to prevent it from reacting negatively with blood which can lead to stroke, data corruption, and sensor blocking.**

Industry solution:
^^If a safely engineered, modern bio-medical chip is designed utilizing precise validation standards (multiple trials conducted) ten the risk of stroke may be reduced to near 0%, data corruption has a 1-5 % decrease in occurring over multiple months, and the risk of the sensor being blocked is minimized by 80-90%.^^

Another example of Chemistry being used in VLSI is by preventing structural flaws in the GaN design for the chip.

The concept of unit 5 reaction rates and mechanisms is crucial in determining the behavior of atoms when they are transmitted into the GaN during CVD (Chemical Vapor Deposition).

These behavioral predictions makes it easier for Electronic Warfare devices to jam intended targets, due to the proper structure of the GaN, which reduces the potential for electromigration, leading to better chip function.

One more connection between an AP Chemistry concept and chip design that I will discuss in this post includes redox electron transfer. 

This unit 4 concept is crucial in the etching part of the design, because it helps predict the behavior of a plasma gas during etching.

**This prediction ensures the chips are etched properly, thereby reducing the probability of cap failure due to non-properly aligned etches, which reduces the potential monetary net loss for the company.
